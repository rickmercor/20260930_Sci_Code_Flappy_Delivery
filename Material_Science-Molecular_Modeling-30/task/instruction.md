## Background

Structural instabilities in anharmonic crystals are governed by the free-energy cost of collective displacements. Quantum and thermal fluctuations can rotate and mix vibrational modes, so an auxiliary harmonic spectrum need not have the same stability as the free energy after fluctuations have relaxed. A Gaussian variational description treats both mean positions and correlated fluctuations as thermodynamic variables. Biasing a mean displacement introduces an external constraint while leaving fluctuations free to equilibrate. Higher-order interactions dress the effective scattering vertices through Gaussian averages, which makes locating the loss of positional stability a coupled equilibrium and response problem.

The motion of an instability boundary with temperature probes how quantum fluctuations alter the balance between stabilizing covariance feedback and destabilizing positional response. This sensitivity includes the changing optimal Gaussian and the changing response operator. Degenerate vibrational frequencies do not make a matrix-valued thermodynamic response ambiguous, but differentiating mode labels separately can miss the continuous response of a mixed eigenspace.
