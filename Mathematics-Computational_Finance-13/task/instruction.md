## Background

Equity-linked and unit-linked life-insurance contracts combine market participation with long-dated guarantees, and remain economically significant: the policyholder's premiums accumulate in a fund tracking an equity index, while the insurer stands behind a minimum benefit at death and at maturity. Many such contracts also grant a right to surrender the guarantee rider at prescribed dates, which converts the valuation into an optimal-stopping problem on top of an already multi-factor pricing problem. Because these liabilities run for one or two decades, their value is sensitive to the joint behaviour of equity returns, volatility, interest rates and policyholder behaviour, and small modelling differences translate into materially different fair premiums and hedge ratios. The literature on this class of products runs from the early work on asset-linked guarantees through the incorporation of stochastic interest rates, surrender and bonus mechanisms, and periodic-premium designs.

From a computational standpoint the valuation is a finite-horizon stochastic dynamic program with a continuous state that does not recombine. Recombining trees represent the financial factors efficiently, but the accumulated fund receives a fixed cash contribution at each anniversary while otherwise evolving multiplicatively, so two paths reaching the same factor state generally hold different fund levels and the fund dimension branches without recombining. A direct grid over all state variables is therefore expensive, and its cost grows quickly with the number of factors and contractual dates. Approximate dynamic programming is the usual response to this kind of state-space growth, most commonly through regression-based simulation such as least-squares Monte Carlo. That route is flexible but, in a stopping problem, superposes three distinct error sources — sampling error, approximation error in the regression basis, and the bias of an estimated stopping rule — which are difficult to separate or to bound.

Singular-point methods offer a deterministic alternative that exploits contractual structure instead of averaging over it. The observation is that the value function is smooth almost everywhere and that its economically meaningful structure is concentrated at a small number of locations: payoff kinks, guarantee levels, and the boundaries between continuation and exercise generated by the backward recursion itself. A representation that retains the function only where such structure exists, and that propagates those locations backwards through the contractual maps, can be far more compact than a fixed global grid while remaining fully deterministic. Applied to the fund dimension, this turns periodic contributions into translations of the function's argument and lets an early-exercise decision be imposed pointwise on the same object, so no additional state variable is needed to record contribution history. The accuracy of such a representation is controlled by an explicit error criterion on the retained approximation rather than by a resolution parameter, and a bound on the represented value also constrains the derivative with respect to the fund, which is what a hedging application requires.

On the modelling side, a constant-volatility, constant-rate specification is inadequate over these horizons. Heston stochastic variance captures leverage and time-varying equity risk through a mean-reverting square-root factor, while a deterministic-shift extension of a square-root short-rate model preserves non-negativity of the rate factor and simultaneously reproduces the observed initial term structure exactly, since the shift $\varphi(t)$ absorbs the difference between the model's own zero-coupon curve and the market's. Both factors are of Cox-Ingersoll-Ross type, so both admit recombining discretisations built in the Lamperti coordinate $\chi_Z = 2\sqrt{Z}/\sigma_Z$, on which the diffusion coefficient is constant and a uniform grid is natural; transition probabilities are then chosen to reproduce the exact one-step conditional moments of the underlying process. Coupling the two factor lattices so that a prescribed correlation is preserved, and advancing the fund conditionally on the resulting joint branches, gives a deterministic backward recursion for the whole contract in which mortality enters as deterministic actuarial weights and the surrender right as a pointwise obstacle.

The supporting theory for schemes of this kind rests on Markov-chain approximation arguments: local consistency of the discrete generator with the limiting diffusion, tightness, and vanishing jumps identify every weak subsequential limit with the solution of the corresponding martingale problem, and uniform integrability then transfers convergence to discounted contract values across a finite set of contractual and exercise dates. Under stronger regularity, a binomial-type discretisation admits a first-order weak-error bound and a Talay-Tubaro asymptotic expansion, which in turn justifies Richardson extrapolation as an acceleration. These deterministic values are typically validated against independent Monte Carlo for the contract without early exercise and against cross-fitted least-squares Monte Carlo once surrender is admitted, the latter serving as an ordering and policy-consistency check rather than as a precise benchmark, since an estimated stopping policy is generally suboptimal and biases the simulated value downwards.

## Problem

A long-dated equity-linked life-insurance contract pays annual premiums into an investment fund and provides guarantees at death and at maturity, with a right to surrender the guarantee rider at prescribed anniversaries. Valuing such a contract under correlated stochastic volatility and stochastic interest rates is a finite-horizon dynamic program whose accumulated-fund state does not recombine, because each anniversary credits a fixed cash amount to a fund that otherwise evolves multiplicatively. Recent work addresses this by discretising only the financial factors on recombining lattices and carrying the fund as a one-dimensional function at each factor node, so that contributions act as translations of the fund argument and surrender is applied as a backward obstacle. Your task is to compute the fair annual premium of one such contract under a fully correlated Heston-CIR++ specification.

Under the risk-neutral measure the equity index $S$, instantaneous variance $V$ and non-negative rate factor $X$ satisfy

$$dS_t=(r_t-q)S_t\,dt+\sqrt{V_t}\,S_t\,dW^S_t,\qquad dV_t=\kappa_V(\theta_V-V_t)\,dt+\sigma_V\sqrt{V_t}\,dW^V_t,$$
$$dX_t=\kappa_r(\theta_r-X_t)\,dt+\sigma_r\sqrt{X_t}\,dW^r_t,\qquad r_t=X_t+\varphi(t),$$

with Brownian correlations $\rho_{SV},\rho_{Sr},\rho_{Vr}$ and a deterministic shift $\varphi$ chosen so that the model reproduces the initial market discount curve exactly at all grid maturities. Between contract dates the accumulated fund $F$ has the same proportional return as $S$, and at each anniversary $i=0,\dots,T-1$ at which the contract is continued the amount $D$ is credited, $F_i^{+}=F_i^{-}+D$. With accumulation rates $g_m$ and $g_s$, write $G_i^{(\gamma)}=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}D\,e^{\gamma(i-j)}$; the maturity benefit is $\max\{F,G_T^{(g_m)}\}$, the benefit on death during policy year $(i,i+1]$ is $\max\{F,G_{i+1}^{(g_m)}\}$ paid at anniversary $i+1$, and the surrender payoff is $R(i,F)=\max\{\alpha_s F,\;G_i^{(g_s)}\}$. Mortality is independent of the financial factors.

Use the following configuration:

- $T = 3$ years, annual premiums and fund contributions, $D = 100$, issue age 50
- $g_m = 0.02$, $g_s = 0.01$, $\alpha_s = 0.95$, dividend yield $q = 0.015$
- $V_0 = 0.04$, $\kappa_V = 2.00$, $\theta_V = 0.04$, $\sigma_V = 0.30$
- $X_0 = 0.018$, $\kappa_r = 0.45$, $\theta_r = 0.025$, $\sigma_r = 0.10$
- $\rho_{SV} = -0.70$, $\rho_{Sr} = -0.20$, $\rho_{Vr} = 0.02$
- one-year death probability $q_x = 1-\exp\!\left(-\left(A + B\,c^{\,x}\right)\right)$ with $A = 5\times10^{-4}$, $B = 1\times10^{-5}$, $c = 1.10$
- initial curve given by the instantaneous forward rate $f(u) = 0.02 + 0.01\left(1-e^{-0.5u}\right)$
- $N_{yr} = 4$ numerical time steps per year
- square-root lattice scale parameters $\lambda_V = 1.0$ and $\lambda_X = 1.4$

The valuation must be deterministic: discretise the variance and rate factors on recombining square-root lattices in the Lamperti coordinate at the stated scales, couple them so that the prescribed factor correlation is reproduced, advance the fund conditionally on each joint factor branch, and carry the value at each joint factor node as a function of the accumulated fund through the backward recursion. The value carried as a function of the fund is a numerical representation, not part of the specification: report the premium converged with respect to it, so that further refinement of that representation would not alter the figures reported. The fair annual premium is the value of $P$ for which the time-zero value of the contract is zero, with premiums payable at anniversaries $0,\dots,T-1$ while the policyholder is alive and the rider remains in force. Report the fair annual premium of the contract with the mixed surrender payoff $R$ above.

### Step 1

cir_transition_stencil

Goal
----
Select the one-step transition stencil at a given node of a recombining square-root lattice. The lattice is uniform in the Lamperti coordinate of the factor, anchored at the initial factor level and stepping by lam*sqrt(h), with the factor level clipped at zero, the lattice floor, wherever the Lamperti coordinate is negative. A selected successor index whose Lamperti coordinate is negative is reported as the lowest index whose coordinate is non-negative. A parent index whose factor level is zero carries no transition and is rejected; every node with a strictly positive level, including the lowest one, is a valid parent. The transition from the parent node must reproduce the exact one-step conditional mean and variance of the square-root factor on three lattice nodes carrying non-negative probabilities. Return the three successor indices relative to the parent, in increasing order, together with their transition probabilities. The chosen triplet is the narrowest one admitting such a distribution, and its span may exceed the immediate neighbours of the parent. Only triplets whose lowest index is at or below the parent index and whose highest index is at or above it are considered, so the parent may itself be the lowest or the highest node of the triplet, and a triplet with a repeated factor level is skipped. When several admissible triplets share the smallest index span, take the one whose outer-pair midpoint is nearest the parent index; if that still ties, take the one with the lower outer index, then the lower middle index.

```python
def cir_transition_stencil(z_root: float, kappa: float, theta: float, sigma: float,
                           lam: float, h: float, j: int) -> np.ndarray:
    '''Select the one-step transition stencil at node j of a square-root lattice.

    The lattice is uniform in the Lamperti coordinate of the square-root factor,
    anchored at z_root and stepping by lam*sqrt(h). The transition from the
    parent node reproduces the exact one-step conditional mean and variance of
    the factor on three lattice nodes carrying non-negative probabilities.

    Parameters
    ----------
    z_root : float
        Strictly positive factor level at which the lattice is anchored.
    kappa : float
        Strictly positive mean-reversion speed.
    theta : float
        Strictly positive long-run level.
    sigma : float
        Strictly positive diffusion coefficient.
    lam : float
        Lattice scale parameter, strictly between 0 and 2.
    h : float
        Strictly positive time step.
    j : int
        Index of the parent node relative to the lattice anchor. The factor
        level at this index must be strictly above the lattice floor.

    Returns
    -------
    out : np.ndarray
        Array of shape (2, 3). Row 0 holds the three successor indices relative
        to j, in increasing order, as floats. Successor indices below the
        lattice floor are clamped to it. Row 1 holds the corresponding
        transition probabilities.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        If the arguments do not describe a valid square-root lattice, if the
        parent node lies at or below the lattice floor, or if no admissible
        stencil exists at the requested node.
    '''
    return out
```
