# Physics-Condensed_Matter_Physics-42

## Background

A microswimmer that steers is not simply a particle that moves. To turn toward
anything it must first hold an internal estimate of which way to turn, and that
estimate is itself a physical degree of freedom: it relaxes at a finite rate,
it is driven by its own noise, and maintaining it costs work. A self-propelled
agent of this kind therefore dissipates on two separate accounts, one for the
torque that turns its body and one for the torque that keeps its internal
estimate pointing where the world says it should point.

The setting for this task is a single such agent in an unbounded plane,
navigating toward one target, in dimensionless units built from its own
self-propulsion speed and the rotational diffusivity of its heading. Its
internal sensor is fast: it relaxes far more quickly than the body turns or the
position changes.

What makes the configuration below more than a textbook exercise is that the
sensor is not equally attentive everywhere. Its alignment rate is a prescribed
function of distance from the target, taking three constant values on three
concentric regions, while the noise it carries does not change with position.
The agent is therefore steered by an estimate whose quality varies across the
plane, and the stationary distribution of where it is found, together with the
rate at which it dissipates, both respond to that variation.

All quantities are dimensionless. Rates are measured in units of the heading's
rotational diffusivity, lengths in units of the self-propulsion speed divided
by that diffusivity, and entropy in units of Boltzmann's constant. The reported
quantity is a single steady-state rate.

## Problem

A point-like agent moves in a plane at constant self-propulsion speed along a heading that undergoes rotational diffusion and is simultaneously turned, with constant steering strength 0.48, toward the direction currently held in a noisy internal sensor. That sensor is itself driven toward the locally preferred direction of a fixed steering policy at a rate much larger than every other rate in the problem, and carries angular noise of its own. The agent's position diffuses as well, and the Peclet number, defined as the squared self-propulsion speed divided by the product of the translational and heading rotational diffusivities, is 2.5; lengths are measured in units of the self-propulsion speed divided by the heading's rotational diffusivity, and times in units of the inverse heading rotational diffusivity.

The steering policy points everywhere toward a single target located at the origin. The sensor's alignment rate is modulated with distance from that target, its unmodulated value being multiplied by zero within radius 0.06, by one half between radius 0.06 and radius 0.55, and by one beyond radius 0.55, while the sensor's angular noise is left unchanged wherever that rate is reduced or absent. The sensor's accuracy - its angular diffusivity divided by the product of the heading's rotational diffusivity and the sensor's unmodulated alignment rate - is 0.30. Both angular couplings, that of the heading to the sensor readout and that of the sensor readout to the policy direction, take the dipolar form given by minus the sine of the difference of the two angles involved.

In the statistical steady state, report the mean rate of entropy production generated by the alignment torque acting on the sensor, in units of Boltzmann's constant times the heading's rotational diffusion rate. The requested value is the one obtained when the agent's positional density is determined from its heading statistics expanded to first order in spatial gradients, with no contribution retained from the two radii at which the alignment rate changes; it is this expansion's value, not the exact stationary value of the full dynamics, that is asked for.
