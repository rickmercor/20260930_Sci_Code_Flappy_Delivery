## Background

Genuine multipartite entanglement need not imply that a state requires an irreducibly multipartite resource: independent bipartite links can already entangle every party. A useful alternative asks whether local processing of lower-party resources and shared classical randomness can prepare the target state.

A constructive approach keeps a feasible mixed network state while improving its approximation to a target density matrix. Pure states generated by a small unitary network supply new directions, and a constrained mixture with a short memory preserves feasibility. Complex resource contractions, coordinate optimization on local unitaries, and the active face of the mixture simplex interact, so a locally improved trial does not imply a globally optimal approximation.

## Problem

Compute the dimensionless sufficient white-noise fraction certified by the Hilbert–Schmidt network-state ball construction after the finite variational calculation below, rather than an exact noise threshold or a positive-entanglement witness. The target is the six-spin Gibbs state at $\beta=0.73$ of $H=-\sum_{j=0}^{5}J_jZ_jZ_{(j+1)\bmod6}-\sum_{j=0}^{5}h_jX_j$, with $J=(1,0.85,1.2,0.9,1.1,0.95)$, $h=(0.79,0.91,1.03,0.83,0.97,1.11)$, $k_B=1$, unit energy scale, standard Pauli matrices, and site 0 the most significant computational-basis bit. Group the sites into $A=(0,1)$, $B=(2,3)$, and $C=(4,5)$, and restrict pure trial states to independent normalized two-qubit resources in the ordered links $A_0B_0$, $B_1C_0$, $C_1A_1$, followed by four-dimensional local unitaries on $A_0A_1$, $B_0B_1$, $C_0C_1$; the output basis is $A_0A_1B_0B_1C_0C_1$.

Start the mixed network approximant at $I_{64}/64$ with empty pure-state memory of capacity four, and process seed labels $k=0,\ldots,11$ with support-sweep counts $(2,0,1,2,0,1,2,0,2,1,0,2)$, independently reinitializing each trial with resource $e=0,1,2$ proportional to $(1,(k+1+e)/10+i(e+1)/7,(2e-k)/9-i/5,0.8-i(k+e)/13)$ and local gate $U_a=e^{-i(k+a+1)\pi P/13}e^{-i(2k-a+2)\pi Q/17}$ for $a=0,1,2$, where $P=X\otimes Y$, $Q=Z\otimes I_2$, and the right factor acts first.
