# Biology-Ecology-51

## Background

Ecological systems observed repeatedly over time trace trajectories through a state space whose geometry is set by the
dissimilarities between their observed states (for community data, typically the Bray-Curtis dissimilarity between
relative species abundances). Units that share the same external conditions and the same internal dynamics follow
analogous trajectories, and the set of such trajectories, an ecological dynamic regime, has been proposed as a
reference for resilience analysis: the deviation of a disturbed unit is measured against the regime rather than
against a single equilibrium. The framework has been descriptive so far. Forecasting the past and future of a unit
observed only once or briefly requires an algorithm that exploits the regime's topology: locating the states of the
reference trajectories that resemble the target, following those trajectories backward and forward in time, and
combining what they show into predicted states, with a neighbourhood radius and distance-dependent weights that
limit the influence of distant trajectories in sparsely sampled regions of the state space. Because such a forecast
cannot be validated against unobserved states, its accuracy has to be assessed from internal consistency: if the
regime determines the target's trajectory, trajectories forecast from the predicted states should pass back through
the observed target, and because such a metric needs no held-out data it can also serve as the criterion for choosing
the neighbourhood radius and the strength of the distance weighting. Synthetic regimes generated by discrete-time community models with successional dynamics, such
as Ricker-type Lotka-Volterra maps in which late-successional species suppress pioneers, provide fully reproducible
test cases in which every trajectory is known and the behaviour of the forecast can be examined exactly.

## Problem
