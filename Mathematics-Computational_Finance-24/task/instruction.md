## Background

Zhang, Wu, Xiao, Gong, and Xu formulate option-model calibration as a two-step surrogate-assisted procedure. The underlying financial model is the fractional volatility stochastic-jump (FVSJ) model, while a Physics-Informed Chebyshev Kolmogorov–Arnold Network (PCKAN) is trained to approximate its option-pricing map. Once trained, the PCKAN is frozen and used as the inexpensive forward model inside differential-evolution calibration.

In the offline pricing stage, European put values are generated from the FVSJ characteristic function using the paper's Fourier-cosine pricing construction. The present benchmark specializes the FVSJ dynamics to the single-factor Heston limit by suppressing the jump component and second volatility factor. The resulting characteristic-function pricing problem retains stochastic variance and correlation and therefore must not be replaced by the Black–Scholes closed-form option formula. The Black–Scholes equation enters the construction separately, as a physics constraint imposed during surrogate training.

The surrogate is a Chebyshev Kolmogorov–Arnold Network. Rather than using conventional fixed scalar activation functions, a CKAN represents its edge functions through trainable Chebyshev expansions. The paper uses Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, with inputs transformed through tanh before the polynomial expansion. The resulting network learns the dependence of option prices on contract variables and model parameters.

The physics-informed version augments supervised fitting to FVSJ-generated option prices with a residual derived from the Black–Scholes partial differential equation. This residual regularizes the learned price surface through its dependence on option value and derivatives with respect to the underlying price and time. It does not redefine the supervised targets as Black–Scholes prices: the pricing labels remain those generated by the FVSJ model.

After offline training, all PCKAN parameters are frozen. Calibration then treats selected financial-model parameters as decision variables and evaluates candidate parameter vectors by passing them through the frozen surrogate for the observed contracts. The calibration objective is the discrepancy between these surrogate prices and the fixed synthetic market prices.

Differential evolution performs this second-stage search without differentiating through the calibration objective. Candidate parameter vectors evolve through mutation, crossover, bound handling, and selection. In the benchmark, only the first-factor mean-reversion rate and long-run variance are calibrated. The required scalar is the minimum sum of squared pricing errors attained by the differential-evolution population after the prescribed final generation.

Thus the benchmark tests the complete scientific chain: stochastic-volatility Fourier-cosine pricing supplies deterministic reference data; the tanh-transformed second-kind Chebyshev KAN learns the pricing map under a physics-informed loss; and differential evolution calibrates financial-model parameters using only the frozen surrogate. Black–Scholes pricing, first-kind Chebyshev polynomials, generic neural-network substitutions, and gradient-based parameter calibration represent different constructions and do not implement the requested framework.

kappa_1 in [0.5, 4.0],

lo + (hi - lo) * rng.random((20, 2)),

lo = [0.5, 0.01]



and



hi = [4.0, 0.12].



Initial objective values are evaluated for all 20 generation-0 population members.



For each evolution generation, targets are processed sequentially in population-index order i = 0,...,19.



For target i, the eligible donor indices consist of every population index except i. Three distinct donor indices are sampled without replacement in one RNG operation. In the sampled order, their current population members define a, b, and c.

v = a + F(b - c),

The deterministic benchmark requires one crossover random draw to be consumed for every coordinate, including the forced coordinate. Therefore the crossover operation is conceptually



for j in range(2):

    crossover_draw = rng.random()

    if crossover_draw < CR or j == jrand:

        trial[j] = mutant[j]

This RNG-consumption rule is part of the benchmark specification because numpy.random.Generator is stateful. Omitting the random draw for jrand changes every subsequent RNG state and therefore changes donor selection, bound repair, crossover, and ultimately the deterministic generation-60 result.

Likewise, short-circuit logic that avoids the random draw on the forced coordinate does not reproduce the specified deterministic trajectory.
