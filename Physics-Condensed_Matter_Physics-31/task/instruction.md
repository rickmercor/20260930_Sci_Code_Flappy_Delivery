## Background

Real-time observables in interacting lattice-fermion systems are difficult to simulate because interactions spread operators across a growing basis. Propagating an observable in an ordered Majorana-string basis separates the restricted mixing caused by quadratic dynamics from the additional sectors generated by interactions. A useful compression retains strings according to their coefficients and the number of unpaired Majoranas, with different allowances inside and at the boundary of a Trotter layer so that temporary excursions are not removed prematurely. Gaussian state preparation can instead be absorbed into an exact transformation of the observable before its occupation-state expectation is evaluated. This yields a controllable finite computational prescription, while the effect of a chosen finite truncation remains distinct from exact many-body dynamics.

## Problem

Use zero-based mode order $m=2s+\sigma$ with $\uparrow=0$, $\downarrow=1$, interleaved Majoranas $\Gamma_{2m}=f_m^{\dagger}+f_m$, $\Gamma_{2m+1}=i(f_m^{\dagger}-f_m)$, and the Hermitian basis $\mu(v)=i^{q(v)}\prod_{a\text{ increasing}}\Gamma_a^{v_a}$, where $q(v)=[w(v)(w(v)-1)/2]\bmod 2$ and $w(v)=\sum_a v_a$.

Apply three identical symmetric second-order layers of duration $\Delta t=0.21$: in state-chronological order the first hopping half-layer visits the two edges as listed, then up and down spin on each edge, splitting each hopping term for modes $i<j$ into its two Majorana generators on supports $(2i,2j+1)$ followed by $(2i+1,2j)$, while the final hopping half-layer reverses that entire microgate sequence. Between those halves, apply the full-duration on-site interaction block in increasing site order, using its up-pair, down-pair, and four-Majorana supports in that order at each site and omitting the scalar phase; each elementary rotation is written as $\exp[-i\theta\mu(v)/2]$, with angles fixed by this Hamiltonian and the stated half/full durations. Evolve $O$ in the Heisenberg picture with the order-consistent unpaired-string cutting prescription: the endpoint cap is $S=2$ on $w_s(v)=\sum_{m=0}^{5}(v_{2m}\mathbin{\oplus}v_{2m+1})$, and inside each layer use the smallest additional unpaired allowance that preserves excursions returning to this endpoint subspace through second nested-commutator order. After each elementary Majorana rotation, first combine all contributions to an identical string, then discard coefficients with magnitude strictly below $\varepsilon=0.0002$ and strings above the temporary unpaired cap; restore the endpoint cap after the complete layer, without coefficient renormalization, and let the resulting operator be $\widetilde O$.

The initial state is $W|1,0,0,1,1,0\rangle$, with $W=\exp[-i\tau_p\sum_{ij}(h_p)_{ij}f_i^{\dagger}f_j]$, $\tau_p=0.23$, and $h_p=B\otimes I_2$, where $B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0.4&1&0.2i\\1&-0.3&0.6\\-0.2i&0.6&0.2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Evaluate the preparation exactly on the observable side, $\langle n|W^{\dagger}\widetilde O W|n\rangle$, with no further coefficient or unpaired truncation; the orientation convention is $W^{\dagger}f_iW=\sum_j[\exp(-i\tau_p h_p)]_{ij}f_j$. Return this dimensionless projected estimate to ten decimal places, without clipping or substituting the exact untruncated quench, and in the reasoning justify the signed propagation, layer allowance, preparation treatment, and Fock contraction, including the coefficient Euclidean norm of $\widetilde O$ and its expectation in the unprepared occupation state. Discuss whether this elementary-rotation cutoff preserves charge conservation, with a scientific justification.
