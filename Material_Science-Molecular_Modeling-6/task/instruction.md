## Background

Linear-scaling electronic structure replaces cubic-cost diagonalization with representations that exploit the near-sightedness of the density matrix. Chromatic superposition states compress every sparse operator of a Kohn-Sham problem onto a handful of colored probing vectors, and a block-Krylov projection carries the overlap inverse square root and the Fermi operator at a cost independent of system size. On a small chain the same construction can be audited exactly: dense references reveal precisely what the chromatic compression preserves and what it discards.

## Problem

```python

    """A: (n, n) symmetric; B: (n, m) block of starting vectors; v: int >= 1.
    Returns (n, k): orthonormal basis of the block-Krylov space
    span{B, AB, ..., A^(v-1)B}, built by flattening the blocks in that order,
    orthonormalizing columns left to right by modified Gram-Schmidt with one
```

```python
def projected_inv_sqrt(S, Q):
    """S: (n, n) symmetric positive definite overlap; Q: (n, k) orthonormal
```
