# What the photocurrent-versus-effective-voltage procedure returns for a cell that generates every charge

## Background

The photocurrent of a thin-film solar cell, the difference between its illuminated and dark current
densities, is often plotted against an effective voltage measured from the bias at which the
photocurrent vanishes, and the ratio of its short-circuit value to its reverse-bias saturation is
read as the probability that a photogenerated bound pair dissociates into free charge. The
construction was introduced for blends in which the field-assisted dissociation of bound pairs
genuinely limited the photocurrent; it has since become a routine diagnostic applied to blends
whose free-charge generation is nearly complete and field independent, where a sub-unity ratio
cannot be a dissociation yield.

In an undoped thin-film cell the free carriers are collected by drift in the built-in field, and
their collection competes with bimolecular recombination in the bulk and with two first-order
processes fixed by the contacts: recombination with the equilibrium charge the contacts inject and
the diffusion of photogenerated minority carriers into the wrong contact. The competition between
extraction and recombination makes the photocurrent voltage dependent, so that a reading at short
circuit normalised to a reading at a finite reverse bias reports how far the collection has
progressed, a transport property that also fixes the fill factor, rather than how much free
charge was generated. Because the same processes act on the injected dark carriers, the light and
dark currents do not superpose, and the bias at which the photocurrent vanishes is not the built-in
voltage.

An exact steady-state drift-diffusion solution of the cell, with the generation prescribed,
separates these effects: it gives the collected fraction and the loss budget at every bias, the
ceiling that remains when transport is ideal, its dependence on the injection barrier and on the
light intensity, and the mobility at which bulk recombination stops dominating the loss. Against
that reference the procedure can be evaluated exactly rather than fitted, and the reverse bias at
which its saturation is read becomes part of what it measures.

This task builds that reference for the source's model cell, applies the source's procedure to it
with complete and with field-dependent generation, accounts for every photogenerated carrier at
short circuit, maps the first-order ceiling against the injection barrier, locates the crossover
mobility and the intensity dependence that distinguish first-order from second-order losses, and
asks for the apparent dissociation probability that the procedure returns for the model cell at
the typical reverse bias.

## The exact reference

The reference is the steady-state drift-diffusion model of the cell: Poisson's equation
eps eps0 psi'' = -q (p - n), the continuity equations dJ_n/dx = q (R - G) and
dJ_p/dx = -q (R - G), and the currents J_n = -q mu_n n psi' + q D_n n' and
J_p = -q mu_p p psi' - q D_p p' with D = mu kT/q, with the contact densities and potentials above.
Discretise on the uniform grid of N intervals (nodes x_i = i d / N, spacing h = d / N): Poisson
with the three-point second difference at every interior node, R and G evaluated at the interior
nodes (the contact nodes hold their fixed densities and generate nothing), the currents on every
interval by the Scharfetter-Gummel fluxes J_n = (q D_n / h) [n_{i+1} B(delta_i) - n_i B(-delta_i)]
and J_p = (q D_p / h) [p_i B(delta_i) - p_{i+1} B(-delta_i)] with delta_i = (psi_{i+1} - psi_i) q / kT
and B(x) = x / (exp(x) - 1), and the divergence at a node equal to the difference of its two
interval fluxes divided by h. Solve the discrete system to machine precision (every residual,
scaled with Poisson in units of kT/q and each continuity equation multiplied by h^2 over q D
times the local density, below 1e-12 or at the roundoff floor) by damped Newton iteration in
psi q/kT, ln n and ln p, started from the dark zero-bias Poisson-Boltzmann equilibrium with the
generation switched on at zero bias and continued in the bias in steps of at most 0.05 V; the
converged discrete state is unique, and the current density of a state is the total
Scharfetter-Gummel current J_n + J_p on the middle interval, which is the same on every interval.

## The procedure and the light metrics

With J_light(V) the current under generation and J_dark(V) the dark current of the same cell,
the photocurrent is J_ph(V) = J_light(V) - J_dark(V). The compensation voltage V_0 is the bias at
which J_ph = 0, located by bisection on (0, V_bi + 0.4 V) to 1e-10 V; the open-circuit voltage
V_oc is the bias at which J_light = 0, located the same way; the maximum-power point is the
maximiser of -V J_light(V) on (0, V_oc), located by golden-section search to 1e-9 V; the
short-circuit current is J_sc = -J_light(0) and the fill factor is FF = P_max / (V_oc J_sc). The
procedure reads the saturation current J_sat,exp = J_ph(V_rev) at a reverse bias V_rev and
returns the apparent dissociation probability P_diss = J_ph(0) / J_sat,exp; the true short-circuit
collected fraction is J_sc / (q G_ex d), and the short-circuit collection efficiency of the free
carriers is that fraction divided by the generation yield at short circuit.

## Field-dependent generation

## Where the photogenerated carriers go
